Synapse Explainer

Most neural networks learn slowly by changing permanent parameters during training. But some systems can also form a short-term memory by temporarily strengthening the connections between active units. The network remembers because a recent experience briefly changes how its next signals travel.

In this interactive explainer, you will teach a small network a new symbolic rule through demonstrations, watch its connections change, and test the rule on an unseen example. You will then introduce a competing rule to reveal memory interference - the moment when learning something new begins to disturb what was just remembered.

What you will explore

Teach it. Test it. Disrupt it.

Begin with two or three demonstrations of a hidden rule. The network forms a temporary memory, then applies what it has learned to a new example. Its answer is shown beside the correct result, so success is visible rather than assumed.

Next, introduce a conflicting demonstration. Watch the earlier memory weaken as the new association grows. This contrast makes the central lesson clear: plastic connections enable rapid adaptation, but a fixed memory can become crowded and uncertain.

Using Fast Weights to Attend to the Recent Past

Fast weights form a temporary, rapidly changing memory alongside the network's slower learned parameters. That gives us a computational starting point for asking how a demonstrated action could leave a short-lived trace for immediate reuse.
